- **FATF #assessment methodology**  The objective of an assessment is two-fold: Assess whether a country has implemented the FATF Recommendations → Technical Compliance (‘TC’) Assess whether the country has achieved sufficient AML/CFT outcomes - Effectiveness
- **FATF assessment methodology** #Technical_compliance methodology mirrors the Recommendations.
	Focus on laws, regulations and institutional arrangements.
	The country is assigned a rating for each Recommendation:
	Compliant
	Largely Compliant
	Partially Compliant
	Non-Compliant
https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/Assessment-ratings.html 

- **Effectiveness** The assessment of #effectiveness is fundamentally different from technical compliance. 
	Question: has a country’s AML/CFT system delivered results?
	Inherent challenges:
	Need to be clear about objectives
	Need for data
	Need to use expert judgment to decide what is enough
All countries are different. For example, a certain number of convictions may be considered as high in the context of country A, but insignificant for country B.
- The Methodology defines the High-Level Objective of AML/CFT/CPF as follows: Financial systems and the broader economy are protected from the threats of money laundering and the financing of terrorism and proliferation, thereby strengthening financial sector integrity and contributing to safety and security.

- **IO.3 Immediate Outcome 1** Money laundering and terrorist financing risks are understood and, where appropriate, actions coordinated domestically to combat money laundering and the financing of terrorism and proliferation
- **IO.3 Immediate Outcome 3** Supervisors appropriately supervise, monitor and regulate financial institutions . . . for compliance with AML/CFT requirements commensurate with their risks.

- **FATF Assessment Process (pre-MER)**  Secretariat of relevant assessment body (FATF or FSRB) forms an assessment team:
	5 to 7 assessors
	Practitioners nominated by their respective government.
	Supported by the Secretariat throughout the process
	Assessed country submits a questionnaire on technical compliance and information on risk & context.
	Assessors review TC submission and submit initial review.
	Assessors prepare the scoping note.

- **Assessed country submits information on effectiveness**
	On-site visit: 
	2 to 3 weeks on average. 
	Meetings with stakeholders from the public and private sectors.
	Members of the civil society may also be invited.
	Assessment team drafts report.
	Face-to-face meeting between assessment team and assessed country.

- **A complete mutual evaluation takes up to 18 months.**  Revised report is disseminated to all members of the Global Network.
	Plenary of relevant assessment body (or bodies) discusses the report, focusing on a limited set of key issues, and adopts it.
	The final report is re-circulated for a quality & consistency review.
	The report is published.

	Background on country’s legal and institutional framework
	ML/TF Risks and Scoping of Higher-Risk Issues
	Assessment of effectiveness (IO-specific)
	Key findings
	Recommended Actions
	Core issues
	Technical Compliance Annex

- **About Ukraine assessments Follow-Up Report (FUR)**  In FATF (Financial Action Task Force) terminology, a FUR report is part of the ongoing review process after a country has already been evaluated in a Mutual Evaluation Report (MER). What it is used for
	-A FUR checks how much progress a country has made in fixing weaknesses identified in its FATF evaluation.
	It mainly updates two things:
		Technical compliance (laws and regulations aligned with the 40 FATF Recommendations)
		Sometimes progress in improving implementation over time
	Where it fits in FATF system
	MER (Mutual Evaluation Report) → full country assessment
	FUR (Follow-Up Report) → progress updates after MER
	Sometimes multiple FURs (1st FUR, 2nd FUR, etc.)
	Countries can be placed in:
		Regular follow-up
		Enhanced follow-up (for higher-risk or weaker systems)"
